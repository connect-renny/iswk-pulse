The role of parenting in education

Yet, long before a child enters a classroom, their future quietly begins taking shape at home.

A child may spend only a few hours each day in school, but the lessons that build character, confidence, emotional strength, values, and attitude towards life are first learned through everyday experiences with family. The warmth of a parent’s reassurance, the patience in their guidance, the encouragement after failure, and the comfort of simply being present become the invisible foundation upon which children build their sense of self.

Long after children forget chapters, worksheets, and examinations, they remember how they were made to feel during their growing years — whether they felt heard, understood, encouraged, accepted, and believed in.

Research across the world consistently highlights one powerful truth: parental involvement has a direct and lasting impact on a child’s success and well-being.

Studies show that children whose parents are positively engaged in their educational journey demonstrate:

  • Higher academic achievement,
  • Stronger emotional resilience,
  • Healthier self-esteem,
  • Improved behaviour and discipline,
  • Better peer relationships,
  • Greater problem-solving abilities,
  • and Stronger motivation towards learning.

More importantly, children who grow up with emotionally connected and involved parents are more likely to develop confidence, emotional security, empathy, and the courage to face life’s challenges with resilience. At the heart of every successful child is usually an adult who chose to believe in them consistently.

Parenting in today’s world is far more complex than ever before. Children are growing up amidst digital distractions, academic competition, social pressures, emotional challenges, and constantly evolving expectations. In such a rapidly changing environment, children need more than supervision — they need emotionally available adults who listen without judgement, guide with patience, establish healthy boundaries, and create safe spaces for conversation, trust, and connection.

Effective parenting today therefore goes beyond monitoring performance; it involves being emotionally responsive, encouraging independence and accountability, supporting balanced academic, social, and emotional development, and modelling values, digital responsibility, empathy, and lifelong learning through everyday actions.

Sometimes, the smallest moments leave the deepest impact.

  • A conversation during dinner

  • Reading together

  • Appreciation for effort instead of only results

  • Setting healthy routines

  • Listening after a difficult day

  • Attending a school programme

These moments build emotional security –
the foundation upon which learning and confidence grow.

These seemingly ordinary moments become extraordinary in a child’s life. They create emotional security — the foundation upon which confidence, learning, and resilience grow.

At Indian School Wadi Kabir (ISWK), we firmly believe that education is not the responsibility of the school alone. True transformation happens when parents and educators work together with trust, empathy, and shared purpose. A strong partnership between home and school creates consistency in values, expectations, emotional support, and guidance — enabling children to flourish not only academically, but also socially, emotionally, and ethically.

The ISWK Parenting Calendar 2026–27 has therefore been envisioned as a bridge that strengthens this invaluable partnership between home and school. Through Parent Connect programmes, parenting workshops, counselling initiatives, well-being sessions, inclusive education support, and engagement activities, we seek to empower parents as active collaborators in their child’s holistic growth journey.

Because every child deserves a strong circle of support — a circle where home and school stand together with trust, empathy, encouragement, and shared purpose. When parents and educators walk hand in hand, children do not merely achieve academic success; they flourish into confident learners, compassionate individuals, emotionally resilient human beings, and responsible citizens prepared to face the future with courage and character.

Parenting Updates, Learning, Support & Engagement

Developmentally Responsive

PULSE recognises that every stage of childhood brings different needs. From Balvatika to Class XII, parents receive age-appropriate guidance, resources and engagement opportunities aligned with their child’s academic, social, emotional and developmental journey.

Structured & Purposeful

PULSE brings together a planned continuum of parent engagement through the ISWK Parenting Calendar 2026–27, ensuring that communication, learning, support and participation are purposeful, timely and connected.

Through Parent Connect Sessions, Skill Builder Workshops, orientations and practical resources, PULSE equips parents with relevant strategies to support learning, wellbeing, positive behaviour, digital responsibility and growing independence.

Wellbeing, Inclusion & Support

PULSE connects families with counselling, wellbeing programmes, A.B.L.E inclusive education support, SEL initiatives and need-based interventions, ensuring that every learner is seen, supported and valued.

Parents are invited to become active contributors to the school community through parent–child activities, volunteering, experiential learning, school initiatives, career guidance and Growth Celebrations.

PULSE provides parents with a central space for updates, advisories, resources, programmes, support services and important school information, helping families remain informed and connected.

Reflection, Feedback & Continuous Improvement

PULSE values the parent voice. Participation, feedback and engagement evidence help the school reflect on impact, respond to emerging needs and continuously strengthen the home–school partnership.

Counselling, Inclusion & Student Support

A.B.L.E Department –

Nurturing Every Child with Care, Compassion and Confidence

At ISWK, we believe that every child deserves opportunities to learn, grow, and succeed in an environment where they feel safe, valued, and supported. The Counselling and Special Education – A.B.L.E Department works collaboratively with students, parents, teachers, and school leaders to promote student wellbeing, inclusive education, and holistic development.

Recognising that learners have diverse strengths, needs, and learning styles, the department provides counselling support, social-emotional learning opportunities, parent guidance, and individualized interventions to help students thrive academically, emotionally, and socially.

Our Support Services

  • Individual and group counselling

  • Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) programmes

  • Learning support and intervention planning

  • Parent guidance and awareness sessions

  • Referrals to external specialists when required

  • Inclusive education support and accommodations

Developmentally Responsive Inclusion
& Wellbeing Practices

Balvatika 1–3

Children are supported through structured routines, sensory experiences, storytelling, music, movement, and play-based learning that promote emotional security, communication, confidence, and positive social interaction.

Grades 1–2

Students develop empathy, self-awareness, and confidence through multisensory learning experiences, buddy systems, storytelling, and guided social interactions. Parent sessions support understanding of developmental needs and learning differences.

Grades 3–5

Learners build social awareness, resilience, and responsible behaviour through discussions, role plays, anti-bullying initiatives, and community-focused activities. Parent awareness programmes encourage early identification and support where required.

Grades 6–8

Students engage in age-appropriate wellbeing programmes focusing on emotional regulation, identity development, mental health awareness, resilience, and positive relationships during adolescence.

Grades 9–10

Support focuses on self-advocacy, inclusion, mental wellbeing, responsible decision-making, and managing academic and personal challenges through counselling, awareness programmes, and student-led initiatives.

Grades 11–12

Students receive guidance related to wellbeing, career planning, future pathways, academic balance, and transition readiness as they prepare for higher education and adulthood.

Coping With Changes

Understanding Curriculum Changes & Supporting Our Parent Community

As education continues to evolve in alignment with NEP 2020 and the National Curriculum Framework, schools are increasingly focusing on competency-based learning, holistic development, wellbeing, and real-world application of knowledge. We understand that such changes may raise questions for parents regarding learning expectations, assessment practices, and how best to support children at home.

At ISWK, we are committed to ensuring that parents feel informed, supported, and actively involved throughout this journey.

How ISWK Supports Parents

  • Clear
    Communication

    Regular circulars, advisories, newsletters, and updates help parents understand educational changes and their implications for student learning.

  • Parent Feedback &
    Consultation

    Surveys, discussions, and feedback platforms provide opportunities for parents to share perspectives, concerns, and suggestions.

  • Parent Orientation &
    Workshops

    Interactive sessions led by educators, counsellors, and specialists help parents better understand learning approaches, assessments, wellbeing, and child development.

  • Parent Connect
    Hours

    Structured discussions encourage meaningful dialogue, collaborative problem-solving, and shared learning among parents and educators.

  • Parent–Teacher Meetings
    (PTMs)

    PTMs provide opportunities to discuss each child’s academic progress, emotional wellbeing, strengths, and support needs.

  • Capacity Building
    for Staff

    Teachers and school leaders continuously strengthen their skills in communication, parent engagement, student wellbeing, and inclusive educational practices.
